## Additional items checked

- **RCW 19.255 (personal information — notice of security breaches):** the chapter file exists in the corpus (`rcw/19_business_regulations—miscellaneous/19.255_personal_information—notice_of_security_breaches.md`). Breach-notification duties are operational duties of a data holder; the EULA neither assumes nor disclaims them. Section-level review was not performed — **unverified** at section level, no EULA change proposed on this basis.
- **Washington My Health My Data Act (chapter 19.373 RCW):** the chapter is not in the local corpus; the second pass researched the enacted text at app.leg.wa.gov and the Attorney General's guidance page (atg.wa.gov/protecting-washingtonians-personal-health-data-and-privacy), accessed 2026-11-08. **Researched verdict: the Act does not regulate Rhetoric's core operation; it bites at exactly one boundary, data a user sends to the Licensor.** Determinations: (1) A voice recording or voice model is within the Act's definitions as data: RCW 19.373.010(4) names voice recordings from which an identifier template can be extracted as biometric data, and RCW 19.373.010(8)(b)(ix) makes biometric data consumer health data without qualification. The voice-biomarker inference route (RCW 19.373.010(8)(b)(xiii)) is dormant because no one processes voice data to infer health status. (2) In purely local use the Licensor performs none of the acts in the definition of collect (RCW 19.373.010(5)) and determines neither the purpose nor the means of processing (RCW 19.373.010(23)); the user does, so the Licensor is not a regulated entity for local processing, and is not a processor either. This reading is strong but untested: the Act contains no on-device exemption, and no court or AG guidance addresses the theory that supplying software is determining the means. (3) The Act has no small-business exemption; the small-business provisions (RCW 19.373.010(28)) only delayed compliance dates that expired in 2024. (4) Support and crash submissions are the trigger point, because receipt is collection. Code verification in the second pass shows those channels are diagnostics-only by design and cannot carry audio, voice models, manuscripts, or rendered audio, so no consumer health data reaches the Licensor in any designed flow; watch items are logs that capture synthesized text (speech content can identify a health condition) and users describing a condition in free-text reports. PRIVACY.md now states the contingent policy that governs if a user sends audio anyway: support-only use, restricted access, no sharing, no sale, deletion at closure or within 30 days after, and the RCW 19.373.040 rights with a 45-day response and an appeal path to the Attorney General. (5) Selling licenses is not a sale of consumer health data under RCW 19.373.070; that regime is contingent only, and no analytics or telemetry exists to trigger it. (6) Geofencing does not apply: the prohibition is RCW 19.373.080 and the definition is RCW 19.373.010(14) (the first-pass tasking cited the definition's section for the prohibition), and the Software implements no geofence and no location detection of any kind, so every element of the prohibition is absent. (7) An EULA click-through could never supply consent under the Act (RCW 19.373.010(6) bars consent obtained through a general terms agreement); Section 5 of the EULA now states expressly that the Agreement is not a RCW 19.373.030 consent or a RCW 19.373.070 sale authorization. Enforcement: a violation is a per se Consumer Protection Act violation (RCW 19.373.090), carrying the CPA private action and penalties; no AG enforcement action under the Act was located as of 2026-11-08 (unverified negative), and the first private case, Maxwell v. Amazon.com, Inc., No. 2:25-cv-00261 (W.D. Wash.), was voluntarily dismissed in May 2025 without a merits ruling.

- **Seller legal name and street address:** the repo identifies the Licensor only as the owner/licensor of Rhetoric with contact codysa90@gmail.com (`LICENSE`, `pyproject.toml` authors). The finalized EULA keeps that identification and puts the seller identity/address in the applicable order or invoice rather than publishing a personal address in the license text. Whether a specific sales channel requires more (e.g., a registered trade name or a business address on invoices) is **unverified** and is an owner/counsel item, not hidden by this memo.

## Second pass — research verdicts and adversarial comb-through (2026-11-08)

The three questions the first pass left unverified were researched to primary sources; rows 2 and 6 and the My Health My Data item above now carry the verdicts. The update lane's proposed update/support language (docs/release/UPDATE_SUPPORT_EULA_LANGUAGE.md) existed by amendment time and was folded into Section 8 in substance. The whole EULA was then combed as hostile counsel. Log (attack — clause — disposition):

1. Missing general severability — Section 18 — FIXED: reformation-then-severance clause added.
2. Liability cap decay: the twelve-month lookback in Section 12 decays to zero in year two of a one-time perpetual license — FIXED: the cap is now the total amount paid under the Agreement, the fee-paid cap form Mortenson enforced.
3. Undefined license term — Sections 1.3 and 1.4 — FIXED: the term is perpetual for the licensed version scope, ending only on Section 14 termination, surrender, or an accepted superseding license.
4. Weasel wording (should provide, should send) — Sections 14 and 16 — FIXED: both now read will.
5. Refund overbreadth against merchant-of-record terms, unwaivable chargeback rights, and EU, UK, Australian, and Brazilian consumer law — Section 7.2 — FIXED by full rewrite (row 2).
6. Stale personality-rights remedies and no forged-digital-likeness coverage — Section 4.2 — FIXED: SSB 5886 (chapter 69, Laws of 2026) language and the RCW 63.60.060 civil penalty added; row 9's remedies statement corrected.
7. Warranty-disclaimer conspicuousness on the actual delivery medium — Sections 7.2, 11, and 17 — STRENGTHENED: the full text is available before purchase at the point of sale and shown again in full at acceptance, and all three sections now say so.
8. Amendment without notice — Section 18 — FIXED: amendments bind future purchases; material changes to an existing license require renewed acceptance under Section 6.2.
9. Entire agreement versus storefront and marketing claims — Section 18 — FIXED in text: the integration clause no longer limits remedies for statements applicable law makes actionable, or Section 7.2(b) rights. 10. Characterization risk if a court calls the transaction a service or a license rather than a sale of goods — Sections 11 and 18 — FIXED: the warranty, remedy, and liability provisions now state they apply under any characterization, including a hybrid transaction under RCW 62A.2-102(2). If Article 2 does not apply, RCW 62A.2-314 does not attach by its own terms and no general Washington common-law implied warranty for services was found; the alternative-operation wording covers that branch.
11. Repair-only remedy versus the RCW 62A.2-719(3) in-state facilities proviso and failure of essential purpose under RCW 62A.2-719(2) — Section 7.2(b) — FIXED by the cure, replacement, refund ladder; whether electronic patch delivery counts as an in-state facility is unverified and is no longer relied on.
12. Battle of the forms — formation — NO TEXT CHANGE: for consumer buyers, RCW 62A.2-207 makes later terms mere proposals, and warranty disclaimers and remedy caps are material alterations in any event (Step-Saver Data Systems, Inc. v. Wyse Technology, 939 F.2d 91 (3d Cir. 1991)). The protection is the Mortenson formation flow — full text before payment and again at acceptance, with the Section 17 acceptance record — which the EULA describes and the order flow must actually implement. Any future channel that completes a sale before the EULA is shown (for example a marketplace selling on its own terms) breaks that flow and needs its own assent capture.
13. 15. Indemnity scope — Section 13 — KEPT: scoped for consumer Licensees in the first pass. Washington's anti-indemnity statute, RCW 4.24.115, is construction-specific on its face; it was not verified against the corpus in either pass, and no broader Washington anti-indemnity norm for software licenses was found. Labeled unverified; no change.
16. Anti-competing-product restriction — Section 3 — KEPT: the clause carries an except-where-prohibited-by-law savings and sits beside the LGPL savings language. Accepted risk, flagged for counsel.
17. Whether a pure download is a tangible consumer product under that Act is unverified; any future written warranty reopens the question.
18. Voice-clone misuse liability allocation — Sections 4 and 13 — KEPT, with the Section 4.2 addition: consent, anti-impersonation, and disclosure duties sit on the Licensee, backed by the indemnity. 20. Article 2A — Section 1 — CHECKED again under the new term language: a perpetual, one-time-paid license is not a term lease under RCW 62A.2A-103(1)(j), and the drafting avoids fixed-term possession and return-of-goods mechanics (row 15 stands).
